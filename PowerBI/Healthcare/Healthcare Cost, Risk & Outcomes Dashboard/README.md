# Healthcare Cost, Risk & Outcomes Dashboard

## 📌 Domain
Healthcare Analytics

---

## Overview
This project analyzes Medicare beneficiary data to understand how **patient risk, hospital utilization, and treatment patterns influence healthcare costs and outcomes**.  
The focus is on identifying which patient groups drive higher costs, where outcomes vary, and how care utilization differs across hospitals and regions.

---

## Business Objective
- Identify patient groups with higher medical cost and clinical risk  
- Analyze readmissions, length of stay, and mortality patterns  
- Compare cost variation across hospitals and regions for similar patient profiles  
- Support analysis aimed at improving care quality while controlling costs  

---

## Dataset
- Source: CMS DE-SynPUF (Sample 0)  
- Type: Synthetic Medicare claims data  
- Time Period: 2008–2010  
- Includes: Patients, admissions, diagnoses, procedures, costs, prescriptions, and facilities  

---

## Key Metrics
- Average Length of Stay (LOS)  
- Readmission Rate  
- Mortality Rate  
- Average Quality Score  
- Total Medical Cost  
- Patient Risk Level (Low to Very High)  
- Prescription Cost and Utilization  

---

## Analysis Performed
- Compared healthcare cost and outcomes across patient risk levels  
- Analyzed readmission and length-of-stay trends by age group and risk category  
- Identified diagnoses and procedures that contribute most to overall cost  
- Compared cost variation across hospitals for similar patient risk profiles  
- Reviewed prescription usage and treatment duration across age groups  
- Mapped cost and patient risk distribution by geographic region  

---

## Key Insights
- High and very high risk patients account for a disproportionately large share of total medical cost and longer hospital stays.  
- Readmission rates increase steadily with patient risk, indicating higher care complexity and follow-up needs.  
- A relatively small set of diagnoses and procedures drives a significant portion of overall healthcare spending.  
- Noticeable cost variation exists across hospitals for patients with similar risk levels, suggesting differences in care practices or efficiency.  
- Older patients show higher prescription usage and longer treatment duration, contributing to higher overall cost.  
- Certain regions consistently exhibit higher patient risk and medical cost compared to others.
